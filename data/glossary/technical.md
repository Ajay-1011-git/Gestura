# Domain glossary — `technical`

Selected for a workshop, lab, IT desk or classroom — a setting where the
conversation is about equipment and procedure rather than people and symptoms.

**Most technical vocabulary is deliberately absent, and that is the design.**
Product names, library names, error codes and version numbers have no
established ISL sign and never will; the correct handling for them is
letter-by-letter fingerspelling (T2.6), which is precisely what a clean miss
here routes them to. Adding them to this table would convert a correct
fingerspelled spelling into a fabricated sign — the exact substitution FR-20
forbids. Entries below are limited to terms with a real, stable ISL sign.

**Where this domain differs from the others.** What a selected domain actually
changes is *which terms resolve at all*: `ambulance` hits under `medical` and
cleanly misses here, `keyboard` hits here and cleanly misses under `medical`.
That hit/miss difference is the mechanism, and it is what the T2.2 verification
exercises.

A few words appear in more than one domain with the same gloss but a different
documented sense — "test" is a procedure run against equipment here and a blood
draw under `medical`; "report" is system output here and a document collected at
a desk there. Worth recording for whoever reviews these files, but be clear that
the shared gloss token means the rendered sign is currently the same either way.
Making those genuinely diverge would need distinct signs in the render lexicon,
which is vocabulary work explicitly out of Stage 2's scope (PRD R-10).
